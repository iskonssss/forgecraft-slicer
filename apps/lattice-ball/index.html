Printing tips (TPU on Bambu A1 mini)
  • Unzip, then open the .3mf in Bambu Studio. It carries these settings on the object: chosen layer height, 3 walls (Arachne), 15% infill, 5 mm brim, and Tree Slim auto supports. Pick your TPU filament profile and feed it from the external spool.
  • Why it's built this way: both airless reference balls are a thick shell (about 11% of the radius on the ping pong ball) cut with small holes, about 53% solid all the way through. The ribs are deep and narrow, not round struts, so the ball springs back instead of squashing.
  • Material matters most. The tennis ball's designer reports about 50% bounce height with TPU 98A or PLA Flex, but only about 20% with TPU 95A.
  • The print time shown is an estimate for Generic TPU (3.2 mm³/s) on an A1 mini, calibrated on the ping pong ball's Bambu Studio time. Supports and brim add a few minutes. Slice once in Bambu Studio to confirm before the class.
  • Inside the ball blocker keeps supports out of the hollow. Around the name keeps support marks off the letters. The ping pong reference printed with no supports at all, just a brim, so it's worth trying that to save time.
  • If Bambu Studio warns about the mesh, use its Fix model option.
Weight is model volume × 1.21 g/cm³ (typical TPU). Print time and weight are estimates, not slicer output.
